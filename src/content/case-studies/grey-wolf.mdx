  <section class="mb-16 prose prose-neutral max-w-3xl dark:prose-invert">
    <h2>The brief linked buying to understanding</h2>
    <p>The four-person team worked from a communications brief with two connected goals: sell clothing and help shoppers understand its material, production, and durability story. Emma, the brief-led persona, represented a shopper looking for longer-lasting clothes without giving up a contemporary style. This was a design assumption, rather than a persona validated through interviews.</p>
    <p>Competitor and moodboard references included Organic Basics, KnowledgeCotton Apparel, and Good On You. The group selected Lora and Source Sans Pro, a 16px base, and a 1.125 type-scale ratio. Huemint supported palette exploration, with contrast adjustments in Leonardo. Shared category cards, product cards, and navigation made the direction usable across separately developed pages.</p>
    <p>Additional button padding supported the mobile focus. The prototype imagery includes AI-generated concept photography; it represents the proposed brand, rather than documented products or customers.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="mb-16 prose prose-neutral max-w-3xl dark:prose-invert">

    <p>That shared navigation gives the separately built pages a common starting point. The implemented Basics link connects to the category page, and product breadcrumbs provide a route back through the collection.</p>

    <div class="prose prose-neutral max-w-none dark:prose-invert">
      <h2>Use native state for a small prototype</h2>
      <p>Radio inputs represent size and color choices. Checkbox-controlled sections expose the menu and product information without a JavaScript application layer. These controls let the prototype demonstrate selection and disclosure within the project’s HTML-and-CSS scope.</p>
      <p>Material information sits alongside the purchase decision rather than being confined to a separate brand page. The interface therefore explores both browsing and evaluating an item.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="prose prose-neutral max-w-none dark:prose-invert">
      <h2>The scope boundary</h2>
      <p>The repository specifies a 390px viewing width. This is a mobile storefront exercise, with no implemented checkout, cart persistence, stock system, or payment processing. Some visible actions and categories remain placeholders.</p>
      <p>The report records HTML validation and CSS minification. It also identifies a collaboration limit: dividing sections of the same codebase became harder to integrate as the project grew. Feature branches and a more modular CSS structure were the team’s recommendations for a subsequent project.</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="mb-16 rounded-2xl bg-neutral-50 p-8 dark:bg-neutral-900">
    <h2 class="mb-4 text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-800 dark:text-neutral-100">Outcome & evaluation boundary</h2>
    <p>The team produced a shared UI kit, Figma prototype, and linked HTML storefront. Time and experience constraints meant no interviews or usability tests were conducted. The brief’s proposed 10% monthly traffic growth after six months and 15% conversion-rate increase within a year were business targets, rather than achieved results.</p>
    <p class="mt-4">A next usability task would ask a shopper to find the socks, select a size and color, locate material information, and return to the category. That would test whether the buying and education goals work together in practice.</p>
